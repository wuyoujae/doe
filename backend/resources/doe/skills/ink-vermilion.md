# Ink & Vermilion

Use for contemporary publishing, considered brand stories, architecture, objects or a precise research narrative. Compose a warm-white page with ink-like type, deliberate asymmetry, fine structure and a small vermilion accent. Give a real object or material detail room to breathe. The Eastern editorial influence comes from proportion, restraint and the relationship of mark to empty space; do not add dragons, seals, bamboo, calligraphy or pagodas unless the actual topic calls for them.

The theme uses #FAF8F3 paper, #161614 headlines, #282723 body and #A82F24 vermilion. EB Garamond provides English editorial headings, Alexandria supplies body text, and MiSans keeps Chinese readable. Use short display phrases; a long Chinese title can use the plain headline role rather than pretending a Latin serif will apply. Card corners are nearly square and the workspace is a muted warm gray. Photographs retain natural color; the theme's accent is selective, not a red filter.

Select page roles from the approved argument. Vary dominant geometry and composition across nearby pages using the current selection policy; a role does not prescribe one component.

## Working rules

Use `theme=yrkie-vermilion` for a new deck unless the user selected another theme. On an existing deck, read its current revision and use SetDeckTheme; do not silently replace the user's choice. Ask about missing density, audience or brand assets through the available question tool before production; respect an already answered brief and outline confirmation. Theme roles supply fonts and colors. Do not invent authorable CSS, absolute coordinates, font fields or custom image masks.

Every icon-capable item gets a meaningful Lucide icon, such as Search, Package, Users, Clock or Check. Keep real dates, counts and percentages in editable text; a component ID containing numbered does not require decorative numerals. Use one dominant visual and related support, not several competing diagrams. A single metric plus two reasons can be stronger than three full panels. Put each heading and its related content in the same stack. Normally use plain level=1 headlines; level=2 supports dense evidence, and display is for short focal statements. Do not default to one title component or a page of bullets.

Low density: one claim, one visual, necessary qualification. Medium: one relationship with 2-4 supporting items. High: prioritize the complete evidence in an open chart/table or clearly grouped material; reduce picture footprint before type. Preserve facts, units, bases, periods, sources, alternatives and uncertainty. Do not shrink a whole block, omit facts or repeat every chart value in prose. A hidden legend hides series names; required units and qualifiers must remain visible elsewhere. For a donut with named categories, normally set showLegend=true explicitly, including compact variants. If intentionally hiding the legend, provide all required category names in adjacent native text; never leave unexplained sectors.

## Images are part of the composition

Carry image art direction throughout the deck, not only on the cover. Choose subject imagery that explains the brief, atmospheric scenes that establish mood, or page-surface artwork around editable content. A well-designed decorative background is a valid inner-page visual; it need not contain a subject photo. Preserve regular visual presence and varied page silhouettes without a fixed image percentage or asset count. Dense evidence pages may remain plain. Respect text/data-only requests and factual-image restrictions; illustration and atmosphere are not documentary evidence.

Plan the role in the Outline with generateimg, ratio and boolean removeBackground. Direct GenerateImage accepts prompt, ratio and optional removeBackground: no style, negativePrompt or aspectRatio fields. Resolve all bracketed placeholders before calling. After generation call ReadImage on the exact returned reference, then place that exact slideReference. A prose promise or a link alone is not an image. Keep all titles, values, chart labels and diagram relationships native and editable.

The default Slide fill is `#FAF8F3`; the outer workspace and card surfaces are different. A matched image with removeBackground=false must request this actual page color uniformly at every joining edge, without edge shadows, texture, gradients, vignette, white mat or frame. An intentional scene photo may retain its setting. A color prompt does not guarantee a pixel-perfect seam. For an isolated complete subject use removeBackground=true: the host adds the matting preset and removes the background; do not write green-screen instructions yourself. Use fit=contain, and inspect real alpha/edge quality rather than claiming transparency from a prompt. Keep natural green material green.

For full backgrounds, inspect the actual quiet zone before setting contentWidth/contentAlign. contentWidth is a fraction of the text safe area, not of image pixels. textSurface=none affects text primitives only; collections retain their reading surfaces. foreground=light makes direct text light; foreground=dark retains theme text colors. Do not combine a full background with edge-image-layout. A normal content image does not require a full-page image or a frame.

## Decorative page-surface art direction

Native editorial type, a clean metric or a simple collection carries the message; the surface adds paper-and-ink character without a poster treatment. Use the actual page fill, not the workspace. Local-asset joining edges stay uniform; an intentional full-page surface may carry subtle material throughout. **Complete decorative-background prompt:** ratio=16:9, removeBackground=false.

> Create an original contemporary editorial paper background, landscape 16:9. Use warm uncoated paper #FAF8F3, with a very fine tactile fiber visible only on close inspection. Keep normalized x=0.06-0.94, y=0.06-0.91 evenly lit and quiet for native editable text and evidence. Along the extreme left x=0.00-0.02, let a short vermilion #A82F24 printed plane enter from the top, ending around y=0.33. Keep the accents sparse, contemporary and precise, with no distressed ink, faux calligraphy, dramatic folds or global shadow. The canvas itself is the paper, not a sheet photographed on a table. Undecorated joining edges match #FAF8F3. No letters, numerals, seal, signature, logo, semantic icon, label, cards, charts, photographs, frame or completed slide. This is a quiet paper-and-ink environment for native editorial composition.

## Cutout quality and background choice

For cutout quality, this guidance supersedes earlier illustrative removal settings and replacement-count advice. Green foliage, sage materials and green reflections can overlap the host's green key: a risk, not guaranteed failure. For green objects or foliage within an editorial photograph, prefer `removeBackground=false`: use a uniform background matching the actual visible slide fill at placement (theme default `#FAF8F3`), or an intentional real scene. Preserve the subject's colors.

Prefer `removeBackground=true` for complete, clearly separated subjects whose visible colors contrast with the host's green key; avoid forcing green subjects through this route. Do not request a textured background, floor, scene or cast/drop shadow; the object's material texture and form shading may remain. The host supplies matting instructions.

Before placement, use `ReadImage` on the exact returned asset. Inspect speckles, gray/green halos, cropped tips and damaged edges at the intended scale. Do not enlarge or place a visibly dirty cutout. Use a matched-background `false` alternative or diagnose the observed defect before targeted regeneration through the normal tool, within existing tool limits and the user's authorized scope. A successful call does not prove clean edges or correct alpha; never claim an unverified cleanup.
